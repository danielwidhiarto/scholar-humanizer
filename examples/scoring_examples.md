## Example 7: Literature Review, Humanized (Score: 42/50)

> Research on social media and academic performance has followed two main lines. The first examines time displacement: hours spent on platforms replace study time, with effect sizes ranging from r = -0.21 to -0.38 across studies (Smith, 2020; Jones, 2021; Lee, 2022). > Social media use was negatively correlated with GPA (r = -0.34, p < 0.01; Table 1). Students reporting more than 3 hours of daily use had a mean GPA of 2.8, compared to 3.3 for those using less than 1 hour (t(340) = 4.21, p < 0.001). ## Scoring Calibration Notes

**35/50 is a revision guide.** A score below 35 prompts a closer review of whichever dimensions need work; it is not a detector result or proof of authorship.

**Common patterns by score range:**

| Range | Typical Issues |
|-------|---------------|
| 10-20 | Vague or ornate phrasing, little concrete substance |
| 21-30 | Mixed — some substance obscured by formulaic phrasing |
| 31-34 | Close but needs tightening — usually one dimension dragging |
| 35-40 | Acceptable — minor polish needed |
| 41-45 | Good — clear, specific claims and appropriate voice |
| 46-50 | Strong against this rubric; still requires field and journal review |

The score is a qualitative editorial aid. A low score may reflect vagueness, weak organization, unnecessary repetition, or an integrity concern; it does not measure whether a text "sounds human," establish authorship, or predict AI-detector outcomes.
