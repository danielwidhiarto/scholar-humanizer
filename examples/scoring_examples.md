## Example 1: AI-Generated Abstract (Score: 18/50)

> This paper explores the intricate and multifaceted relationship between artificial intelligence and modern healthcare systems. In an era of unprecedented technological advancement, it is imperative to understand the nuanced ways in which AI is revolutionizing patient care. This comprehensive study leverages a holistic approach to examine the vibrant landscape of AI-driven medical innovations. The findings offer valuable insights and pave the way for transformative changes in the field.

| Dimension | Score | Reasoning |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Precision | 2 | No specific claims, no methodology, no findings |
| Voice | 3 | Reads like ChatGPT, not a researcher |
| Flow | 4 | All sentences same length and structure |
| Economy | 2 | Nearly every word is filler |
| Integrity | 7 | No fabrication, but no substance either |
| **Total** | **18** | **Below threshold — revise** |

## Example 3: Methods Section with Passive Voice (Score: 39/50)

> Participants were recruited through university mailing lists and social media advertisements. A total of 342 students completed the online survey, which measured daily social media use, study habits, and academic self-efficacy. The survey was piloted with 15 students to assess item clarity. Data were analyzed using SPSS version 28, with mediation analysis conducted using the PROCESS macro (Hayes, 2022).

| Dimension | Score | Reasoning |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Precision | 8 | Specific sample size, tools, procedures |
| Voice | 7 | Appropriate academic register for Methods |
| Flow | 7 | Functional, could vary slightly |
| Economy | 9 | No filler, all substance |
| Integrity | 8 | Claims are verifiable |
| **Total** | **39** | **Above threshold — passive voice is correct here** |

**Note:** A general humanizer would flag "were recruited," "was piloted," "were analyzed" as passive voice to fix. That would be wrong — passive is standard in Methods.

## Example 7: Literature Review, Humanized (Score: 42/50)

> Research on social media and academic performance has followed two main lines. The first examines time displacement: hours spent on platforms replace study time, with effect sizes ranging from r = -0.21 to -0.38 across studies (Smith, 2020; Jones, 2021; Lee, 2022). The second examines mechanism: social media disrupts attention and sleep, which indirectly affects learning (Chen, 2023; Park et al., 2023). Few studies have examined both lines simultaneously, and none have tested study habits as a mediator — the focus of the present study.

## Example 9: Results, Humanized (Score: 44/50)

> Social media use was negatively correlated with GPA (r = -0.34, p < 0.01; Table 1). Students reporting more than 3 hours of daily use had a mean GPA of 2.8, compared to 3.3 for those using less than 1 hour (t(340) = 4.21, p < 0.001). Study time mediated this relationship: the indirect effect was -0.18 (95% CI: -0.24 to -0.12), while the direct effect was not significant (β = -0.09, p = 0.12; Table 2). The mediation model explained 28% of the variance in GPA.

## Scoring Calibration Notes

**35 is the threshold.** A score below 35 means the text has significant AI-isms or lacks substance.

**Common patterns by score range:**

| Range | Typical Issues |
|-------|---------------|
| 10-20 | Heavy AI vocabulary, no substance, all filler |
| 21-30 | Mixed — some substance buried under AI patterns |
| 31-34 | Close but needs tightening — usually one dimension dragging |
| 35-40 | Acceptable — minor polish needed |
| 41-45 | Good — reads human, clear claims |
| 46-50 | Excellent — publishable quality |

**The score reflects both "sounds human" AND "has substance."** A text can sound human but score low if it's vague (low Precision). A text can be substantive but score low if it's full of AI tells (low Voice).
